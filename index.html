EMI vs SIP vs GST: The 3 Calculators Every Indian Should Bookmark

Three numbers shape almost every Indian household's finances: the EMI going out, the SIP going in, and the GST hiding inside every bill. Most people guess at all three. This guide shows you how to calculate each one properly — and why these three calculators deserve a permanent spot in your bookmarks.

1. EMI: the number that decides your loan

EMI — Equated Monthly Instalment — is what you pay the bank every month for a home, car, or personal loan. The bank spreads your principal plus all the interest over the loan tenure, so you pay the same amount every month.

Here's why you should never skip the calculation. Take a ₹50 lakh home loan for 20 years at 9% interest. The EMI comes to roughly ₹45,000 a month. Over 20 years, you'll pay about ₹58 lakh in interest alone — more than the loan itself. Now shorten the tenure to 15 years. The EMI rises to roughly ₹50,700, but total interest drops to about ₹41 lakh. Five extra years of tenure costs you ₹17 lakh.

An EMI calculator lets you test tenures, compare a 8.75% offer from one bank against 9.1% from another, and see exactly how much a part-prepayment saves you.

When to use it: before signing any loan, when comparing bank offers, and once a year to check whether a prepayment makes sense.

2. SIP: the habit that builds wealth quietly

A Systematic Investment Plan is the mirror image of an EMI — instead of paying the bank every month, you pay yourself. A fixed amount leaves your account monthly and goes into a mutual fund. The power comes from compounding plus rupee-cost averaging: you automatically buy more units when markets dip and fewer when they peak.

Invest ₹10,000 a month for 10 years at an assumed 12% annual return, and you'd put in ₹12 lakh total — but the corpus would grow to roughly ₹23 lakh. Nearly half of it is growth, not your own money. Stretch that to 20 years and the corpus approaches ₹1 crore on ₹24 lakh invested.

A SIP calculator turns vague goals ("I want ₹50 lakh for my child's education in 12 years") into a concrete monthly number.

When to use it: when setting any financial goal with a deadline — education, a house down payment, retirement.

3. GST: the tax you pay without noticing

GST is baked into almost everything you buy, which is why most people can't tell you how much of their spending is tax. The rate depends on the product or service category.

A GST calculator answers two everyday questions. First: how much tax is inside this price? Enter the amount and it splits it into base price plus GST. Second: what's the final price? Enter the base price and it adds the correct GST on top.

On a ₹1 lakh purchase, the difference between GST slabs is thousands of rupees — worth verifying rather than trusting the invoice blindly.

When to use it: before big purchases, when checking invoices, and whenever you're quoted a price "plus GST."

How the three connect

Here's the monthly loop of a well-run household budget: your salary arrives, the EMI calculator has already told you the loan costs are under control, the SIP calculator has set your monthly investment, the GST calculator keeps your spending honest — and the income tax calculator makes sure you're not overpaying the government in between. Run all four once a quarter, and there are no financial surprises left.
